3n+1 cycles in logarithms

Multiplicative Ledger

The additive ledger says a cycle's ascents and descents cancel. Written multiplicatively the same cancellation becomes an exact equation, 2B = ∏(3 + 1/nᵢ), and in logarithms it says the halving count B must be an integer within a hair of L·log₂3. How thin the hair is depends on how large the members are; how far the integers can be from L·log₂3 is decided by the continued fraction of log₂3. Putting the two together is the classical lower bound on the length of a second cycle. This page draws it.

1 · The product around a loop identity · proved

One odd step with x halvings is 2x·n′ = 3n + 1 = n·(3 + 1/n). Multiply these around a cycle and every member appears once on each side, so they cancel:

2B = ∏ᵢ (3 + 1/nᵢ)   ⟺   B = L·log₂3 + Σᵢ log₂(1 + 1/(3nᵢ))

For 1 → 4 → 2 → 1: 2² = 3 + 1/1. Along an open trajectory the same product runs as 2Bk·nk/n₀ = ∏i<k(3 + 1/nᵢ), and in logarithms Bk − k·log₂3 = log₂(n₀/nk) + ck with ck = Σ log₂(1 + 1/(3nᵢ)) ≥ 0: the halvings so far, minus the budget log₂3 per odd step, equal the doublings lost since the start plus a small positive correction. A cycle returns to n₀, so it ends exactly at B − L·log₂3 = cL.

Bk − k·log₂3, halvings above budget log₂(n₀/nk), doublings lost the gap between them is ck, always positive

2 · How thin the hair is proved · cited

Squeeze. Every correction term is positive, so B > L·log₂3 — that is 2B > 3L, T7 on the main page. And each term is at most the one for the smallest member m: 0 < B − L·log₂3 ≤ L·log₂(1 + 1/(3m)). Dividing by L:
0 < B/L − log₂3 ≤ δ(m) = log₂(1 + 1/(3m)) < 1/(3m·ln 2)
Barina's verification (J. Supercomputing 81, 2025) puts every member of a second cycle above 271, so δ < 2.04 × 10−22: the ratio B/L would have to sit above log₂3 by less than two parts in 1022.
Where such ratios live. If some length L admits a B with B/L that close, then the fraction above log₂3 with denominator ≤ L that is closest to it is at least as close, and that fraction is by definition a best upper approximation of log₂3. These are the convergents and intermediate fractions of its continued fraction [1; 1, 1, 2, 2, 3, 1, 5, 2, 23, 2, 2, 1, 1, 55, …]: 2/1, 5/3, 8/5, 27/17, 46/29, 65/41, 149/94, …. So the smallest possible L is the first denominator in that list whose fraction is within δ of log₂3. Nothing else can be a cycle length below it.

3b · Circuits, where "m ≤ 68" lives cited · computed

Simons and de Weger's theorem is not a bound on the length. A cycle's odd steps come in circuits — a run of rises followed by a run of falls, one per local maximum — and m counts them. For each m, two bounds on the number of odd members K fight. A floor: each circuit carries its own squeeze, so the whole cycle's (K+B)/K must sit in a window just above log₂3, and the floor climbs rung by rung up the same staircase of best upper approximations as the chart above (Eliahou's method, Hercher's Theorems 16–27). A ceiling: Baker's theorem on linear forms in logarithms gives K < 1.4784·m·(log₂3)m (S&dW 2010, Thm 3, for 91 ≤ m ≤ 515 619). Where the floor passes the ceiling, that m is impossible. Simons and de Weger cleared m ≤ 68 in 2005 (Acta Arith. 117) and m ≤ 75 in the 2010 revision; Hercher cleared m ≤ 91 in 2023. At m = 92 the floor stops a factor 1.7 short of the ceiling.

4 · What the number says, and what it does not cited

  • A second cycle has at least 72 057 431 991 odd steps and 114 208 327 604 halvings — 186 billion steps of T in all — with every member above 271. Its ratio B/L is one specific fraction, 114 208 327 604 / 72 057 431 991, or a later best upper approximation.
  • This is the Crandall–Eliahou squeeze (Crandall 1978; Eliahou, Discrete Math. 118, 1993), the elementary half of the cycle-length story. Hercher (J. Integer Sequences 26, 2023, Cor. 29) gets one rung further, L ≥ 137 528 045 312 — his 1.375 × 1011 — with the local-maximum structure of Simons and de Weger, Baker-type bounds and a certificate computation whose condition, verification to 3·269, Barina's 2025 bound meets. His floor is the next point on this very staircase: 217 976 794 617 / 137 528 045 312. The squeeze allows the rung 72 057 431 991 (his own "for all m" row, 7.20 × 1010), the machinery removes it, and the next best upper approximation becomes the floor. The repository's exact re-run of his ladder (hercher.py) reproduces both rungs. Nothing here is new; the page draws the classical argument from the ledger.
  • Why the argument stops. Move the slider past 71 and the bound keeps climbing, but only because the verification bound is assumed to. The squeeze converts verified size into forbidden length; it cannot forbid all lengths, because log₂3 is irrational and best approximations exist at every scale. A proof would need B − L·log₂3 to be bounded below by something larger than L/(3m) for all large L — which is Baker-type territory, and even there the known bounds fall short.
  • The ledger, closed. Section 1 of the additive page said ascents equal descents; this page says the same thing as 2B = ∏(3 + 1/nᵢ). The additive form gives the residue gates at the ends; the multiplicative form gives the length bound. Neither closes the loop, and both are exact.
