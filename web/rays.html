The 2-adic skeleton of the Collatz graph

Halving Rays

Every even number sits on exactly one ray y·2x with y odd. Halving slides a number down its ray toward the base y; the 3n+1 step lifts an odd base onto some other ray. Rays for the odd roots 1–19 are drawn below; the rings are where 3n+1 lands, colored by whether the reduced odd S(n) = (3n+1)/2x is larger or smaller than the n that jumped.

ray y·2x, 3 ∤ y dead-end ray, 3 | y — nothing ever lands on it rise: the reduced odd is larger, S(n) > n — lands at height 1 fall: the reduced odd is smaller, S(n) < n — lands at height ≥ 2 fixed point 1 → 4 → 1

Landing pads alternate along every live ray: y·2x ≡ 1 (mod 3) exactly when y ≡ 1 (mod 3) and x is even, or y ≡ 2 (mod 3) and x is odd. A root divisible by 3 never satisfies it — multiples of 3 have no odd predecessor — so those three rays are drawn dashed. Bases 15 and 19 jump to the rays of 23 and 29, which lie outside the drawn set.

Rise or fall is decided by the height alone. One halving gives S(n) = (3n+1)/2 > n; two or more give S(n) ≤ (3n+1)/4 < n for n > 1. So every rising jump lands in the x = 1 column — the sources n ≡ 3 (mod 4) — and every pad at height x ≥ 2 is a descent; the only equality is the fixed point 1 → 4 → 1.
