3n+1 cycles as a balance sheet

Cycle Ledger

Every 3n+1 step is a credit of 2n+1; every halving is a debit of half the number. A trajectory closes into a cycle exactly when the credits and debits cancel, and for 3n+1 the only balance anyone has ever seen is 1 → 4 → 2 → 1. This page keeps that ledger, pairs each odd n with the descent that follows it, reads off what the largest and smallest members of any other cycle would have to look like, and hands the finite version of the question to a SAT solver running in this page — with an honest account of what that can and cannot settle.

1 · The ledger identity · proved

Start at an odd n. The odd step n → 3n+1 ascends by 2n+1; each halving m → m/2 descends by m/2. Your example: 13 → 40 ascends 27, then 40 → 20 → 10 → 5 descends 20+10+5 = 35, net −8 = 5 − 13. Around a closed cycle the nets sum to zero, so Σ ascents = Σ descents. Every start tried so far ends in the same three-step balance at 1.

ascent + (2n+1), the 3n+1 step descent − m/2, one halving odd member the closed cycle
The ledger as a table
Identity. Let a cycle have odd members n₁…n_L (sum O), even members with sum E, and B halvings. Summing S(nᵢ) − nᵢ around the cycle gives 0, i.e. Σ(2nᵢ+1) = Σ descents = E/2. So E = 4·O + 2L: the even members weigh exactly four times the odd ones plus 2L. For 1 → 4 → 2 → 1: 6 = 4 + 2. It is a consequence of closing, not an extra test — but it is the linear consequence, the row an integer program would carry; closure itself is the nonlinear part.

2 · Pairs (n, D(n)) structure

Pair each odd n with its descending sum D(n) = (3n+1)(1 − 2−x), x = v₂(3n+1). The pairs lie on a fan of lines with slopes 3/2, 9/4, 21/8, 45/16, … (one per height x), all below the limiting slope 3. The ascent 2n+1 is a single dashed line. A point below it is a rise (its successor is larger), above it a fall; the vertical distance to the line is n − S(n), and a cycle is a set of points whose distances cancel. Only n = 1, sitting exactly on the line, has ever done so.

rise: D(n) < 2n+1, height 1, n ≡ 3 (mod 4) fall: D(n) > 2n+1, height ≥ 2, n ≡ 1 (mod 4) the fixed point n = 1, on the height-2 line

3 · What another cycle would have to look like proved · lean-checked at both ends

Suppose 3n+1 had a second cycle. Its largest member M is entered by a rise and left by a fall; its smallest member m the other way round. A rise is exactly one halving and a fall at least two, one halving is a congruence mod 4, and being landed on at all is a congruence mod 3. That is the whole content of the gates:

Maximum. Leaving: S(M) < M forces v₂(3M+1) ≥ 2, so M ≡ 1 (mod 4) (T1). Entering: the in-step is one halving, 2M = 3y+1, so M ≡ 2 (mod 3) (T2). Together M ≡ 5 (mod 12) (T3). One 3-adic level deeper, y itself must be landable, so M ≢ 5 (mod 9): M ≡ 17 or 29 (mod 36) (T4). One 2-adic level deeper, M ≢ 9 (mod 16): M ≡ 5, 17, 29 (mod 48) (T8). In binary: M = …01₂ and M ≠ …1001₂.
Minimum. Entering: the predecessor is larger, so the in-step has ≥ 2 halvings. Leaving: the out-step is one halving (two would give S(m) ≤ (3m+1)/4 < m), so 3m+1 ≡ 2 (mod 4), i.e. m ≡ 3 (mod 4); and 3 ∤ m because m is landed on (T0). So m ≡ 7 or 11 (mod 12). In binary: m = …11₂. This is the mirror of docs/STRUCTURE.md, Lean-checked as min_bb_out.
Odd residues mod 12
max ≡ 5 (mod 12) — refined to 17, 29 (mod 36) and 5, 17, 29 (mod 48) · min ≡ 7 or 11 (mod 12) · classes 3 and 9 hold no cycle member at all · the trivial cycle {1} is exempt: it has no rise into it and no fall out of it

And beyond the gates, what is known.

  • Every member exceeds 271 ≈ 2.4 × 1021 — Barina's verification of convergence below that bound (J. Supercomputing 81, 2025).
  • More than 1.375 × 1011 odd members — Hercher (J. Integer Sequences 26, 2023, Cor. 29), made unconditional by Barina's bound.
  • At least 92 local maxima — Hercher 2023, Thm 23: no cycle with 91 or fewer.
  • Halvings outnumber odd steps by more than log₂3 to 1, since M(2B − 3L) > 0 (T7); and at least 41.5 % of the odd steps are single halvings (docs/STRUCTURE.md).
  • The deeper 3-adic gates on M — the sieve with survivor counts a_k — live on the main page.

4 · As a SAT problem computed · MiniSat in this page

"There is a 3n+1 cycle with L odd members, all below 2W" is a finite statement, and finite statements can be written as clauses. The encoding below does exactly that, with the 2-adic gates as clauses and the 3-adic gates as circuits; the solver is MiniSat, running in your browser. Every box it can afford comes back unsat above L = 1 — as it must, since every member of a second cycle exceeds 271 and the page runs at W ≤ 26. At L = 1 it finds the one fixed point, 1. The point of running it is to see the formulation work, and to see its scale.

  • Variables. nᵢ as W bits (n₀ the maximum); tᵢ = 3nᵢ+1 as W+2 bits through two ripple-carry adders; one-hot yi,k meaning "tᵢ has exactly k trailing zeros".
  • Clauses. nᵢ odd; yi,k ⇒ the low k bits of tᵢ are 0, bit k is 1, and ni+1 = tᵢ ≫ k (indices mod L); exactly one k per step; nᵢ < n₀ strictly for i ≥ 1, which fixes the rotation and forbids a shorter cycle run twice.
  • 2-adic gates as clauses. T1 is one unit clause (bit 1 of n₀ is 0); T8 is one 3-literal clause (not …1001₂); T2's "one halving into M" is one clause behind "n₀ > 1"; the minimum's m ≡ 3 (mod 4) is one clause saying some other member has bit 1 set. A condition mod 2a is a clause on a bits: free.
  • 3-adic gates as circuits. A residue mod 3k is not local in binary, so it is computed: a one-hot automaton reads the W bits from the top, state s → (2s + bit) mod 3k, 3k states per bit position, two transition clauses per state and an at-most-one. With it: T0 on every member (final state not 0 mod 3), and on n₀ the sieve of the main page at depths 1 … d — {2, 8} mod 9 is T2 + T4, {2, 17, 20, 26} mod 27 is D2, six classes mod 81 is D3, then 13 classes mod 243 and 22 mod 729 — each behind its magnitude threshold (M > 1, 1, 9, 9, 86) so the box stays exact. Cost: a 2-adic gate is one clause; a 3-adic gate at depth d is about 5·3d+1·W clauses. At W = 16 the six-member box grows from 7 739 clauses to 15 290 at depth 3 and 67 906 at depth 5. "Candidate maxima" lists every odd n < 2W the gate clauses alone accept, checked against arithmetic: below 216, 369 of 32 768 odd numbers, 1.1 %.
  • Enumeration. After each model the value of n₀ is blocked; for fixed L the maximum determines the cycle, so each cycle would appear once.

What this settles, and what it cannot.

  
    
    
  • Inside a box, everything. The encoding is sound and complete for (L, W): its models are exactly the cycles with L odd members and maximum below 2W.
      The Python twin (collatz_maxodd/satcycles.py, CaDiCaL through python-sat) is regression-tested against exhaustive search on 20 boxes, the residue automaton against arithmetic, and the 3-adic gates on boxes that contain cycles (they change nothing there, as theorems must); the ability to find a cycle can only be tested where cycles exist, so that harness runs the same encoder on the maps 3n+q that have them. This page runs 3n+1 only.
    • 
    
  • Outside a box, nothing. The box that could matter has L > 1.375 × 1011 and W > 71: about 1014 clauses. "Unsat" for every smaller box was already known from Barina's verification and the Diophantine bounds on 2B/3L.
      Bounded model checking never closes an unbounded question.
    • 
    
  • Where SAT has actually helped. Yolcu, Aaronson and Heule (CADE 2021; J. Automated Reasoning 2023) turned the conjecture into termination of a string-rewriting system and used SAT to search for
      termination certificates (matrix interpretations) — a proof, not a counterexample. It proved nontrivial weakenings, not Collatz. That is the direction with any life in it: encode the search for an inductive invariant, not for a cycle.
    • 
    
  • The balance as a constraint. Section 1's identity is implied by the closure clauses; on its own it only says Σ S(nᵢ) = Σ nᵢ, weaker than closure. It belongs to an integer program, not a SAT instance, and adds nothing the solver does not already propagate.
