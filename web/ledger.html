3n+q cycles as a balance sheet

Cycle Ledger

Every 3n+q step is a credit of 2n+q; every halving is a debit of half the number. A trajectory closes into a cycle exactly when the credits and debits cancel. This page keeps that ledger, pairs each odd n with the descent that follows it, reads off the residue gates that the largest and smallest members must pass, and then hands the whole question to a SAT solver running in this page — with an honest account of what that can and cannot settle.

1 · The ledger identity · proved

Start at an odd n. The odd step n → 3n+q ascends by 2n+q; each halving m → m/2 descends by m/2. Your example: 13 → 40 ascends 27, then 40 → 20 → 10 → 5 descends 20+10+5 = 35, net −8 = 5 − 13. Around a closed cycle the nets sum to zero, so Σ ascents = Σ descents.

ascent + (2n+q), the 3n+q step descent − m/2, one halving odd member the closed cycle
The ledger as a table
Identity. Let a cycle have odd members n₁…n_L (sum O), even members with sum E, and B halvings. Summing S(nᵢ) − nᵢ around the cycle gives 0, i.e. Σ(2nᵢ+q) = Σ descents = E/2. So E = 4·O + 2Lq: the even members weigh exactly four times the odd ones plus 2Lq. Checked on all 2 127 known cycles with q ≤ 999, M ≤ 20 000. It is a consequence of closing, not an extra test — but it is the linear consequence, the row an integer program would carry; closure itself is the nonlinear part.

2 · Pairs (n, D(n)) structure

Pair each odd n with its descending sum D(n) = (3n+q)(1 − 2−x), x = v₂(3n+q). The pairs lie on a fan of lines with slopes 3/2, 9/4, 21/8, 45/16, … (one per height x), all below the limiting slope 3. The ascent 2n+q is a single dashed line. A point below it is a rise (its successor is larger), above it a fall; the vertical distance to the line is n − S(n), and a cycle is a set of points whose distances cancel.

3 · The gates at the top and the bottom proved · lean-checked at both ends

The largest member M is entered by a rise and left by a fall; the smallest member m the other way round. A rise is exactly one halving and a fall at least two (for members ≥ q), and one halving is a congruence mod 4 while landing at all is a congruence mod 3. That is the whole content of the gates:

Maximum. Leaving: S(M) ≤ M forces v₂(3M+q) ≥ 2, so M ≡ q (mod 4) — no hypothesis (T1). Entering: if M > q the in-step is one halving, 2M = 3y+q, so M ≡ 2q (mod 3) (T2). Together M ≡ 5q (mod 12) (T3). For q = 1: M ≡ 5 (mod 12). Deeper 3-adic levels (T4, D2, D3, the sieve) live on the main page.
Minimum (L ≥ 2). Entering: the predecessor is larger, so the in-step has ≥ 2 halvings — no hypothesis. Leaving: if m ≥ q the out-step is one halving (two would give S(m) ≤ (3m+q)/4 ≤ m), so 3m+q ≡ 2 (mod 4), i.e. m ≡ q+2 (mod 4); and 3 ∤ m because m is landed on (T0). For q = 1: m ≡ 7 or 11 (mod 12). This is the mirror of docs/STRUCTURE.md, Lean-checked as min_bb_out; the census here gives 434/434 with m ≥ q and 1 681/1 681 entries.

4 · As a SAT problem computed · MiniSat in this page

"There is an S_q-cycle with L odd members, all below 2W" is a finite statement, and finite statements can be written as clauses. The encoding below does exactly that; the solver is MiniSat, running in your browser. Every model it returns is checked arithmetically and compared with an exhaustive search of the same box.

  • Variables. nᵢ as W bits (n₀ the maximum); tᵢ = 3nᵢ+q as W+2 bits through two ripple-carry adders; one-hot yi,k meaning "tᵢ has exactly k trailing zeros".
  • Clauses. nᵢ odd; yi,k ⇒ the low k bits of tᵢ are 0, bit k is 1, and ni+1 = tᵢ ≫ k (indices mod L); exactly one k per step; nᵢ < n₀ strictly for i ≥ 1, which fixes the rotation and forbids a shorter cycle run twice.
  • Gates as clauses. T1 is one unit clause on bit 1 of n₀; T2 is one clause behind a comparator n₀ > q; for q = 1, T8 (M ≢ 9 mod 16) is one 3-literal clause. Every 2-adic gate is local in binary. The 3-adic gates — T0, the mod-3 half of T2, T4, the sieve — are not: each needs a mod-3 circuit. A bit-level encoding sees the 2-adic half of the problem for free and the 3-adic half not at all.
  • Enumeration. After each model the value of n₀ is blocked; for fixed L the maximum determines the cycle, so each cycle appears once.

What this settles, and what it cannot.

  
    
    
  • Inside a box, everything. The encoding is sound and complete for (L, W): its models are exactly the cycles with L odd members and maximum below 2W.
      The Python twin (collatz_maxodd/satcycles.py, CaDiCaL through python-sat) agrees with exhaustive search on every box tested — 20 regression tests, including the two 17-cycles of 3n+5.
    • 
    
  • Outside a box, nothing. A nontrivial 3n+1 cycle has more than 1.375 × 1011 odd members (Hercher 2023, Cor. 29, made unconditional by Barina's 2025 verification below 271)
      and a minimum above 271. The box that could matter has L > 1011 and W > 71: about 1014 clauses, and "unsat" for any smaller box was already known.
      Bounded model checking never closes an unbounded question; the cycle bounds come from Diophantine approximation of log₂3, not from search.
    • 
    
  • Where SAT has actually helped. Yolcu, Aaronson and Heule (CADE 2021; J. Automated Reasoning 2023) turned the conjecture into termination of a string-rewriting system and used SAT to search for
      termination certificates (matrix interpretations) — a proof, not a counterexample. It proved nontrivial weakenings, not Collatz. That is the direction with any life in it: encode the search for an inductive invariant, not for a cycle.
    • 
    
  • The balance as a constraint. Section 1's identity is implied by the closure clauses; on its own it only says Σ S(nᵢ) = Σ nᵢ, weaker than closure. It belongs to an integer program, not a SAT instance, and adds nothing the solver does not already propagate.
